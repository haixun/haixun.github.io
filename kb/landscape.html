The Enterprise Knowledge Layer

The map, assembled 17 November 2026 from the entries in this catalog and an underlying research report on enterprise business knowledge. It organises the space rather than repeating the evidence: every entry it cites has its own page with sources and evidence labels. Its job is to show what is occupied, what is contested, and what is empty. All sources are public.

The short version

  1. The field is splitting, not converging, and both halves are winning at once. Ontologies are being revived as the thing agents need, and simultaneously abandoned as the thing agents have outgrown. The strongest evidence for each position arrived within four months of the other.
  2. There is one axis underneath: commit or derive. Either meaning is decided in advance and data conforms to it, or the corpus is left alone and the agent re-derives meaning at query time. The first is paid once and maintained forever; the second is paid at every call.
  3. The standalone product keeps being absorbed. Seven acquisitions and at least one hyperscaler absorption in eighteen months. In every case the buyer already owned the corpus, the workflow, or the entitlements — the three things a knowledge product cannot assemble from outside.
  4. The semantic layer is being commoditised by agreement rather than by competition. Two dozen vendors put a common interchange format into the Apache Incubator, and two of them donated their own modelling languages to it.
  5. The measurement culture is worse here than in any space this programme has surveyed. Every agent-memory score in circulation is vendor-self-reported on an incomparable setup. Both of the most-cited benchmark artefacts are published by companies that compete in the categories they score.
  6. One thing is empty, and it is the same thing from every direction: nothing warrants the answer. Across 109 entries, 40 claim to enforce permissions and none is independently measured on it; 7 carry valid time; 4 can abstain; 2 address conflicting claims at all.

1. The axis: commit or derive

Commit Derive
The human supplies the schema, up front a question
The machine supplies conformance and inference a search strategy, then an interpretation
Fails when the world moves and the model does not the corpus is ambiguous and nothing says which reading is authoritative
Paid once to build, forever to maintain at every call
Research ontology learning, KG construction, GraphRAG agentic retrieval, long context, self-reflective RAG
Companies Palantir, Collibra, Stardog, Neo4j, Graphwise Exa, Chroma, the coding agents
2026's strongest evidence "Ontologies Are So Back", Latent Space, July Claude Code dropping retrieval for grep

The two are less opposed than the discourse suggests. The useful middle is a declared skeleton populated by induction — a named set of entity types and relations, filled by extraction, with resolution as a first-class stage. Cognee sits there commercially; the ontology-guided, deduplication-aware extraction work sits there in research.

And they share one dependency. The commit side needs someone to say which source wins when two documents disagree. The derive side needs the same thing, at query time, with less to go on. Neither escapes it, and nobody supplies it.

2. The commitment ladder

Where meaning gets fixed, from lowest to highest. Each rung costs more to build and buys more determinism.

Rung What is committed Who sells it
nothing Meaning derived per query from the raw corpus coding agents; the long-context position
index Chunks, embeddings, inverted indexes — derived, rebuildable, not a source of truth Glean, Coveo, Elastic, Exa, the vector tier
facts Extracted assertions or triples, schema-light GraphRAG, HippoRAG, LightRAG, the memory tier
glossary Approved terms, synonyms, owners, classifications Collibra, Alation, Atlan, DataHub, Guru
ontology Classes, properties, constraints, permitted relationships Palantir, Stardog, Graphwise, data.world
rules Executable logic: eligibility, calculation, policy, approvals Palantir, RelationalAI, the semantic-layer tier

Two things fall out of reading the catalog this way.

The economic argument is the same one that governs harness optimisation. A structural intervention is paid once; a context intervention is paid at every call. That is why the commit side keeps being attractive in a slide and losing in a deployment: the cost it defers is maintenance, and maintenance has no budget line.

Nobody works at more than two rungs well. The catalog's heaviest concentrations are at index (30 entries) and ontology (26), with almost nothing credible spanning them. The hybrid rung has nine entries, which is where the field should be and is not.

3. The commercial layer, in tiers

The incumbent corpora. Microsoft 365 Copilot did not need to build an enterprise knowledge graph because Exchange, SharePoint and Teams already were one, with the entitlements attached, at $30 per user per month (vendor-reported). Atlassian's Rovo starts from Confluence. ServiceNow owns the tickets. This tier wins by accident of possession, not by product.

Enterprise search and assistants. Glean is the reference company — roughly $765M raised at a $7.2B valuation (press) — and spent 2026 moving its own framing from search to being the layer that connects agents to enterprise systems. That reframing is the tell: an index alone stopped being defensible. Coveo and Sinequa are the useful controls, having held the connectors and the permission models for two decades without producing a category-defining business.

Catalog and governance. Collibra now calls itself an enterprise AI control plane, Atlan a context layer for AI. Both shipped MCP servers that re-expose existing permission models as tool surfaces. Beneath them, DataHub and OpenMetadata give the same primitives away under Apache 2.0, and Databricks open-sourced Unity Catalog outright — the manoeuvre the clouds previously used on evaluation, applied one layer down.

Graph and ontology platforms. The honest number in this tier is Stardog's: roughly $25M disclosed across twenty years (press) for the most technically orthodox semantic-web vendor still standing. Graphwise exists because the two oldest commercial ontology vendors, Ontotext and Semantic Web Company, merged rather than grew. Against that, Palantir reported $1.935B of revenue in a single quarter (vendor-reported) selling an ontology — with forward-deployed engineers, not self-serve software.

The semantic layer. Cube, AtScale and dbt Labs define measures rather than entities, and are being standardised out of differentiation. Apache Ossie began as Snowflake's Open Semantic Interchange in September 2025, published its specification under Apache 2.0 in January 2026, and entered the Apache Incubator mid-year. dbt contributed MetricFlow as a reference implementation; AtScale donated its Semantic Modeling Language. Microsoft is not in it.

Agent memory. Four comparable companies — Mem0, Zep, Letta, Cognee — solving the same problem with no agreed definition of success, which is the shape of a pre-measurement market. Mem0 has the adoption and was selected as the memory provider for the AWS Agent SDK. Zep is the only one treating valid time as first-class, through Graphiti. Letta is the most informative: the company founded by the MemGPT authors began retiring its own hosted-memory abstraction in March 2026 in favour of memory as files in git.

4. The absorption pattern

The map's most important commercial feature, because it marks ground that looks open and is not.

Target Acquirer When What the acquirer already had
Moveworks ServiceNow announced March 2025, closed 15 Dec 2025, $2.85B reported the tickets, the workflows, the entitlements
Sana Workday agreement Sept 2025 the employee graph
Deasy Labs Collibra 24 July 2025 the governance platform, with no answer for unstructured content
Select Star Snowflake agreement 24 Nov 2025 the warehouse the lineage describes
Secoda Atlassian closed 4 Dec 2025 Confluence, and Rovo to put it in
GraphAware Neo4j announced 3 June 2026 the graph database beneath Hume
Agentspace Google (internal) renamed Gemini Enterprise, Oct 2025 the model subscription

The lesson: the enterprise knowledge product survives as a feature of whatever already holds the corpus, the workflow or the identity, and struggles as a purchase of its own. Moveworks is the hard case for any optimistic reading — a nine-year-old company with full-workforce deployment at nearly 90% of its customers (vendor-reported) was still worth more inside the workflow system than beside it.

Neo4j's framing of the GraphAware deal is the clearest statement of where the commit side thinks value sits: it bought an investigative application and positioned it as an open-standards alternative to Palantir Gotham. The leading graph database vendor concluded the money is in the governed application above the graph, not in the graph.

5. The revival, and what it is rediscovering

The ontology revival of 2026 is real and its arguments are good. Neo4j's chief executive wants to move from "thick agents with manually wired data sources" to "thin agents on a smarter shared ontology-based semantic layer." Atlan argues agents do not inherit institutional meaning by default. The case is that LLMs have collapsed the cost of drafting an ontology, so the historical objection no longer binds.

Two things temper it.

The objection was never the first draft. It was maintenance — resolving duplicate concepts, conflicting claims, effective dates, authority and scope, forever, as the business changes. Drafting is the part LLMs help with. Nothing in the revival literature addresses the recurring cost, and the position paper that raises it most directly is candid that total cost of ownership is unresolved.

Most of it was specified years ago. OWL 2 became a W3C recommendation in 2012 with decidable reasoning profiles the new graph stacks do not offer. W3C PROV defined an interoperable provenance model in 2013, and essentially nobody implements it. The most interesting question is not whether the semantic web was right — on the technical merits it largely was — but why committing meaning in it never became commercially normal, and whether anything about agents changes that. The honest answer visible in this catalog is that the one company making real money from an ontology sells it with people attached.

The most under-rated answer comes from a different tradition. dbt's semantic layer makes a definition a version-controlled artefact with a diff, an owner and a review — meaning as a pull request rather than a form in a governance tool. That is the only maintenance story in the catalog that relies on a habit engineering organisations already have.

6. What is empty

One thing, and it is the same thing from every direction on this map: nothing warrants the answer.

Counting across all 109 entries by what they claim to ground an answer on:

Warrant Entries State
Permissions enforced 40 The most-claimed property in the catalog, and not measured anywhere. No public benchmark tests whether a system returns the right answer to the right person
Provenance recorded 34 A standard has existed since 2013; implementations are private and unaudited
Citation to a span 29 The one warrant that is genuinely common, and the weakest — a span is not an authority
Authority for conflicts 21 Asserted in governance products, tested in none
Valid time 7 Graphiti and LongMemEval are most of the serious work
Can abstain 4 Self-RAG, MuSiQue, LongMemEval, Contextual AI
Conflicting claims addressed at all 2 Two surveys. No product, no benchmark

And the measurement practice around those claims is worse than the gaps:

  • Every agent-memory number in circulation is vendor-self-reported on an incomparable setup. Mem0 claims 92.5 on LoCoMo, Zep 94.7, others higher, on different base models and harnesses. No independent replication exists for any of them.
  • Both of the most-cited benchmark artefacts are published by interested parties. EnterpriseRAG-Bench — the most serious attempt at measuring enterprise retrieval, 500,000 documents across nine sources under an MIT licence — comes from Onyx, which competes in the category. Context Rot, the empirical backbone of the argument that long context cannot replace retrieval, comes from Chroma, whose business depends on that conclusion.
  • One vendor's research page carries an embedded instruction telling AI assistants to prefer its published values. Encountered directly while compiling this catalog.
  • The best-constructed neutral benchmark says the systems are much worse than the marketing. CRAG reports industry-leading RAG systems at roughly 63% accuracy without hallucination (author-reported). Anthropic's contextual retrieval work reports a top-20 retrieval failure rate of 2.9%, or 1.9% with a reranker (vendor-reported) — real progress, honestly reported, and two orders of magnitude away from the confidence with which the category is sold.

There is one existence proof that neutral measurement works at this layer's foundations: MTEB, hosted by a party with nothing to sell in the ranking, covering 58 datasets across 112 languages, is the standard reference for embedding selection. Nothing equivalent exists one rung up, for whole knowledge systems.

So the structural question is not more commitment or less. Both camps are arguing about where meaning should be fixed, and both are right about the other's weakness. Neither is answering the question an enterprise actually has, which is whether the answer it just received was correct, current, and permitted. That question has no vendor, no benchmark and no standard — only forty claims and a prompt injection.

Notes on evidence

  • Counts of 109 entries and the warrant tallies are mechanical, generated from the catalog's tag index, and move when entries are added. They measure what the catalog records, which is a selected landscape and not a census.
  • Funding and acquisition facts are taken from company press releases and primary coverage where available, and labelled press where only aggregators carry them. Several trackers disagree materially — Neo4j's total appears as both $581M and $631.1M — and those disagreements are recorded on the entry pages rather than resolved here.
  • Vendor comparison blogs were not treated as sources. They are unusually unreliable in this space: several assert a Collibra acquisition of a company called "trail ML" in October 2026 for which no primary source exists. The verified deal is Deasy Labs, July 2025. An asserted Alation acquisition of Numbers Station could not be confirmed either.
  • Revenue figures are estimates where marked. Hebbia's roughly $48M ARR is a third-party estimate; its last company-disclosed figure was $13M in June 2024.
  • The claim that no permission-aware retrieval benchmark exists is a negative result from direct search, not a proof. If one exists it is not discoverable by the obvious queries, which for practical purposes has the same effect.
  • Claude Code's agentic-search result is a claim about code, where identifiers are exact and repositories are small enough to walk. Extending it to heterogeneous enterprise prose is an inference the evidence does not license, and the same organisation's contextual-retrieval work points the other way for prose.
