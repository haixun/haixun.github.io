The text for today

14

Durante varios meses se empeñó en demostrar el acierto de sus conjeturas.

For several months he worked hard to demonstrate the truth of his idea.

15

Exploró palmo a palmo la región, inclusive el fondo del río, arrastrando los dos lingotes de hierro y recitando en voz alta el conjuro de Melquíades.

He explored every inch of the region, even the riverbed, dragging the two iron ingots along and reciting Melquíades’ incantation aloud.

16

Lo único que logró desenterrar fue una armadura del siglo XV con todas sus partes soldadas por un cascote de óxido, cuyo interior tenía la resonancia hueca de un enorme calabazo lleno de piedras.

The only thing he succeeded in doing was to unearth a suit of fifteenth-century armor which had all of its pieces soldered together with rust and inside of which there was the hollow resonance of an enormous stone-filled gourd.

17

Cuando José Arcadio Buendía y los cuatro hombres de su expedición lograron desarticular la armadura, encontraron dentro un esqueleto calcificado que llevaba colgado en el cuello un relicario de cobre con un rizo de mujer.

When José Arcadio Buendía and the four men of his expedition managed to take the armor apart, they found inside a calcified skeleton with a copper locket containing a woman’s hair around its neck.

Pulling it together

The paragraph in five scenes

  1. The memory (sentence 1): firing squad, father, ice.
  2. The village (2 and 3): Macondo, the river, a world without names.
  3. The gypsies (4 to 8): the yearly visit, the magnet, Melquíades, the demonstration, his pitch.
  4. The bargain (9 to 13): the idea, the warning, the trade, Úrsula, the promise of gold.
  5. The search (14 to 17): months of digging, the armour, the skeleton, the curl.

The verbs tell the story

Read just the preterites in order and you have the plot: llevó · llevaron · se presentó · hizo · fue · se espantó · pensó · previno · cambió · consiguió · replicó · se empeñó · exploró · logró · fue · lograron · encontraron. Everything else is description hung on those seventeen pegs.

Words that come back

llevar (1, 5, 17) · conocer (1, 4) · piedras and enorme (2, 16) · río (2, 15) · arrastrando (7, 15) · aquella (1, 9) · haber de (1, 13) · mujer (12, 17) · hombre (10, 17). Noticing the echoes ties distant sentences together in memory.

Pronunciation

Respellings such as a-BEE-a are rough guides for an English speaker, with the stressed syllable in capitals. Trust the recording over the respelling.

Shadow, then solo

1. Play the whole paragraph at 0.75× and shadow it without stopping. 2. Do it again at normal speed; fall behind if you must, but keep going. 3. Read it alone. The narrator takes about four minutes. A comfortable first target for you is under six.

Mark your breaths

Breathe at full stops and at the commas where the narrator pauses. If you run out of air in sentence 7, go back to the five-breath plan in Lesson 9.

Record yourself

Use your phone's voice recorder for one full reading, then listen while following the text. Note three words that sound least like the narrator and drill only those.
