Grammar and expressions

Fue de casa en casa

fue is the preterite of ir (to go): "he went". It is completely irregular and worth learning as a word on its own. de casa en casa, "from house to house", follows the pattern de X en X.

The gerund: arrastrando

Replace -ar with -ando and you have the "-ing" form: arrastrar → arrastrando, "dragging"; later tratar → tratando, "trying". It describes an action going on alongside the main one.

todo el mundo se espantó

todo el mundo, literally "all the world", is the everyday way to say "everybody", and it takes a singular verb. espantarse is to take fright: se espantó, "was terrified".

al ver que...

al + infinitive means "on doing" or "when ... did": al ver, "on seeing".

A list with articles

los calderos, las pailas, las tenazas y los anafes: every item keeps its own article, los for masculine plural and las for feminine plural. Four kitchen objects: cauldrons, pans, tongs, braziers. paila and anafe are regional, homely words.

se caían, crujían

Both imperfect, because the falling and creaking go on and on as he walks. caerse de is to fall off or down from somewhere: se caían de su sitio, "were falling from their place".

por la desesperación de...

Here por gives the cause: the timbers creaked because of the desperation of the nails and screws.

tratando de desenclavarse

tratar de + infinitive means "to try to". desenclavarse is built in layers: des- (un-) + en- + clav (from clavo, nail, which you have just read) + -ar + -se (themselves): "to un-nail themselves".

Pronunciation

Respellings such as a-BEE-a are rough guides for an English speaker, with the stressed syllable in capitals. Trust the recording over the respelling.

j

j is always the breathy h-sound you met in gitanos: crujían kroo-HEE-an.

Stacked vowels

caían is three syllables, ka-EE-an; fue is one, fweh. The accent mark on í is what splits the vowels apart.

Long words: build from the end

Say ción, then ra-ción, pe-ra-ción, ses-pe-ra-ción, de-ses-pe-ra-ción. Do the same with des-en-cla-var-se and a-rras-tran-do. Each syllable gets equal time.

Other points

  • tornillos: ll as y, tor-NEE-yos.
  • tenazas: te-NA-sas.
  • metálicos: stress on the accented á.
  • espantó: stress on the final ó.

Memorise

A walk through a house. Picture Melquíades going from door to door, and place each clause in a room: the street (Fue de casa en casa arrastrando dos lingotes metálicos), the doorway (y todo el mundo se espantó), the kitchen (los calderos, las pailas, las tenazas y los anafes se caían de su sitio), the walls and roof (las maderas crujían...).

The kitchen list alternates los, las, las, los. Notice the symmetry and it stops being hard.
