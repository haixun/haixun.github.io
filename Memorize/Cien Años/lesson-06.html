No new text today. You now hold the opening movement of the paragraph: the memory, the village, the nameless world, the gypsies, the magnet. This lesson makes it secure before the long sentences begin.

The text for today

1

MUCHOS años después, frente al pelotón de fusilamiento, el coronel Aureliano Buendía había de recordar aquella tarde remota en que su padre lo llevó a conocer el hielo.

Many years later, as he faced the firing squad, Colonel Aureliano Buendía was to remember that distant afternoon when his father took him to discover ice.

2

Macondo era entonces una aldea de veinte casas de barro y cañabrava construidas a la orilla de un río de aguas diáfanas que se precipitaban por un lecho de piedras pulidas, blancas y enormes como huevos prehistóricos.

At that time Macondo was a village of twenty adobe houses, built on the bank of a river of clear water that ran along a bed of polished stones, which were white and enormous, like prehistoric eggs.

3

El mundo era tan reciente, que muchas cosas carecían de nombre, y para mencionarlas había que señalarlas con el dedo.

The world was so recent that many things lacked names, and in order to indicate them it was necessary to point.

4

Todos los años, por el mes de marzo, una familia de gitanos desarrapados plantaba su carpa cerca de la aldea, y con un grande alboroto de pitos y timbales daban a conocer los nuevos inventos.

Every year during the month of March a family of ragged gypsies would set up their tents near the village, and with a great uproar of pipes and kettledrums they would display new inventions.

5

Primero llevaron el imán.

First they brought the magnet.

Pulling it together

The two past tenses so far

Imperfect: background, description, habitPreterite: single completed events
había de recordar, era (twice), se precipitaban, carecían, había que, plantaba, daban llevó, llevaron

Eight imperfects and two preterites: the opening is nearly all scenery. From sentence 6 on, the preterite takes over as things start to happen.

Small words you now know

de (of), a (to), en (in), por (along, around), para (in order to), con (with), y (and), que (that), como (like); the articles el, la, los, las, un, una; and the contraction al. These few words make up over a third of the whole paragraph.

Agreement at a glance

muchos años · aquella tarde remota · aguas diáfanas · piedras pulidas, blancas y enormes · huevos prehistóricos · muchas cosas · gitanos desarrapados · los nuevos inventos. In each pair, check that you can say why the endings match.

Pronunciation

Respellings such as a-BEE-a are rough guides for an English speaker, with the stressed syllable in capitals. Trust the recording over the respelling.

Even syllables

English stretches stressed syllables and swallows the rest. Spanish gives every syllable nearly the same length, like steady taps on a table, with the stressed one only a little louder. Tap a finger for each syllable of Ma-con-do e-ra en-ton-ces u-na al-de-a and keep the taps even.

Breath groups

Play the five sentences and notice where the narrator pauses: after después, after fusilamiento, after pulidas, after reciente, after marzo, after aldea. Inside each group the words run together without a break. Mark those pauses in your mind and breathe only there.

Shadowing

Set the speed to 0.75× and speak along with the recording, a fraction of a second behind the narrator, like an echo. Do not stop to fix mistakes. Three passes of shadowing do more for rhythm than ten careful solo readings.
