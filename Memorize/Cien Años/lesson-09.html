Grammar and expressions

aun

Without an accent, aun means "even": y aun los objetos perdidos, "and even the lost objects". (With an accent, aún means "still".)

los objetos perdidos

perdidos is the past participle of perder (to lose), used as an adjective and agreeing with objetos. Participles of -ar verbs end in -ado (buscado), those of -er and -ir verbs in -ido (perdido).

desde hacía mucho tiempo

Literally "since it-made much time". Spanish measures elapsed time with hacer: the objects had been lost for a long time. Learn it as one block.

por donde más se les había buscado

The hardest phrase in the paragraph, so take it slowly:

  • por donde: "around where", "in the places where";
  • más: "most";
  • se ... había buscado: había buscado is "had searched", and se makes it impersonal, "one had searched";
  • les: "for them", the lost objects.

All together: "in the very places where people had searched for them most".

se arrastraban en desbandada turbulenta

arrastrar was "to drag" in the first half. With se it becomes "to drag oneself along, to crawl". en desbandada is "in a rout", like a crowd scattering.

detrás de los fierros mágicos

detrás de, "behind". fierros is a Latin American form of hierros, "irons". The same two bars are called lingotes metálicos at the start of the sentence and fierros mágicos at the end: from the metalworker's word to the villagers' word.

Pronunciation

Respellings such as a-BEE-a are rough guides for an English speaker, with the stressed syllable in capitals. Trust the recording over the respelling.

Sounds to watch

  • aun: one syllable, awn.
  • objetos: j as h, ob-HEH-tos.
  • hacía: silent h, three syllables, a-SEE-a.
  • mágicos: g before i as h, MA-hee-kos.
  • fierros: rolled rr, FYEH-rros.
  • detrás: stress on the last syllable.

A breathing plan for all of sentence 7

Memorise

Four times y. After the opening clause, the sentence is four "ands": people (y todo el mundo), wood (y las maderas), lost things (y aun los objetos perdidos), the procession (y se arrastraban). Count them on four fingers as you recite.

Practise the five breath groups separately, then two at a time, then all five.
