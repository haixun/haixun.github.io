The telescope, and a prophecy

← Lesson 21Lesson 23 →

Objectives

  • Understand sentences 4 to 6.
  • Recognise two new tenses in Melquíades' speech: the present perfect (ha eliminado) and the future (podrá).
  • Use the "personal a" and sin + infinitive.

For five reales the villagers look through the telescope. Then Melquíades makes a prediction that the twentieth century fulfilled: one day people will see anywhere on earth without leaving home.

Today's text

4

Mediante el pago de cinco reales, la gente se asomaba al catalejo y veía a la gitana al alcance de su mano.

For the price of five reales, people could look into the telescope and see the gypsy woman an arm’s length away.

5

«La ciencia ha eliminado las distancias», pregonaba Melquíades.

“Science has eliminated distance,” Melquíades proclaimed.

6

«Dentro de poco, el hombre podrá ver lo que ocurre en cualquier lugar de la tierra, sin moverse de su casa».

“In a short time, man will be able to see what is happening in any place in the world without leaving his own house.”

Grammar and expressions

la gente se asomaba ... y veía

la gente, "people", is a singular noun in Spanish, so its verbs are singular. Both are imperfect because this went on all day, customer after customer. asomarse a is to put your face to something in order to look.

veía a la gitana: the personal a

When the direct object of a verb is a particular person, Spanish puts a in front of it. It is not translated: veía a la gitana, "saw the gypsy woman".

al alcance de su mano

"Within reach of their hand." su refers back to la gente.

ha eliminado: the present perfect

ha (has) + a participle in -ado or -ido, exactly like English "has eliminated". You met ha in ha de sobrarnos, and había + participle in se les había buscado.

podrá ver: the future

The future is formed by adding endings to the infinitive; -á is "he, she, it will". poder (to be able) is slightly irregular: podrá, "will be able to". el hombre here means mankind.

lo que ocurre en cualquier lugar de la tierra

lo que, "what", as in lo que él mismo llamaba. cualquier + noun is "any ... at all". la tierra here is the whole earth; in paragraph 1 it was the ground.

sin moverse

After a preposition Spanish uses the infinitive where English uses "-ing": sin moverse, "without moving".

  • gente: g before e as breathy h, HEN-te.
  • veía: be-EE-a.
  • ciencia: two syllables, SYEN-sya.
  • podrá: stress on the last syllable.
  • cualquier: kwal-KYER.

Memorise

Pay, look, see. Sentence 4 follows the customer: the payment, the eye at the telescope, the gypsy within reach.

The two quotations are worth knowing as a pair: a claim about now (ha eliminado) and a promise about the future (podrá ver).
