The text for today

9

José Arcadio Buendía, cuya desaforada imaginación iba siempre más lejos que el ingenio de la naturaleza, y aun más allá del milagro y la magia, pensó que era posible servirse de aquella invención inútil para desentrañar el oro de la tierra.

José Arcadio Buendía, whose unbridled imagination always went beyond the genius of nature and even beyond miracles and magic, thought that it would be possible to make use of that useless invention to extract gold from the bowels of the earth.

10

Melquíades, que era un hombre honrado, le previno: «Para eso no sirve».

Melquíades, who was an honest man, warned him: “It won’t work for that.”

11

Pero José Arcadio Buendía no creía en aquel tiempo en la honradez de los gitanos, así que cambió su mulo y una partida de chivos por los dos lingotes imantados.

But José Arcadio Buendía at that time did not believe in the honesty of gypsies, so he traded his mule and a pair of goats for the two magnetized ingots.

12

Úrsula Iguarán, su mujer, que contaba con aquellos animales para ensanchar el desmedrado patrimonio doméstico, no consiguió disuadirlo.

Úrsula Iguarán, his wife, who relied on those animals to increase their poor domestic holdings, was unable to dissuade him.

13

«Muy pronto ha de sobrarnos oro para empedrar la casa», replicó su marido.

“Very soon we’ll have gold enough and more to pave the floors of the house,” her husband replied.

Pulling it together

The rule of position: before a conjugated verb, attached to the end of an infinitive.

haber in four disguises

había de recordar (was to) · había que señalarlas (it was necessary to) · se les había buscado (had searched) · ha de sobrarnos (is bound to). One verb, haber, carries all four.

Adjective in front, adjective behind

truculenta demostración pública · desaforada imaginación · áspero acento · desmedrado patrimonio doméstico. The word in front is the storyteller's colouring; the word behind is the plain fact.

Pronunciation

Respellings such as a-BEE-a are rough guides for an English speaker, with the stressed syllable in capitals. Trust the recording over the respelling.

Speed and evenness

Play sentence 9 and count how long the narrator takes. Read it yourself against a clock. You are not trying to be faster; you are trying to be as even, with no pile-ups at hard words. Any word that trips you, look it up in the vocabulary reference, say its syllables five times, and reinsert it.

The h-sound family

Say these in a row, all with the same breathy sound: José, gitano, imaginación, ingenio, magia, mujer, objetos, crujían, mágicos. And these with silent h: había, hielo, huevos, hizo, hacía, hombre, honrado.

Review

First, sentences 9 to 13: play, shadow at 0.75×, read alone. Then read sentences 1 to 13 from the top without stopping, text visible. Note which sentence feels weakest and give it five minutes of its own.

Memorise

Who speaks next? The scene is a three-way exchange. Fix the order of the people and each sentence follows: José Arcadio Buendía (idea) → Melquíades (warning) → Pero José Arcadio Buendía (trade) → Úrsula Iguarán (objection) → su marido (reply).
