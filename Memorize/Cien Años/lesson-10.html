Grammar and expressions

Quotation marks and dashes

Spanish books use angle quotes, « », for speech. A remark by the narrator inside the speech is set off with long dashes: —pregonaba el gitano con áspero acento—. The quotation simply continues after the second dash.

Las cosas tienen vida propia

tienen is "they have", present tense of tener. propia after a noun means "of one's own": vida propia, "a life of their own". Note Las cosas: Spanish uses the article when speaking of things in general.

pregonaba el gitano

pregonar is what a town crier does: to proclaim in the street. The subject comes after the verb, which is usual with verbs of saying. con áspero acento: "with a harsh accent"; again the adjective in front, for colour.

todo es cuestión de...

es is "is" (present of ser; era was its imperfect). ser cuestión de + infinitive: "to be a matter of doing".

despertarles el ánima

Literally "to wake to-them the soul". Where English says "their soul", Spanish often uses a pronoun for the owner (les, "to them") and a plain article for the thing. les is attached to the infinitive, like las in mencionarlas.

ánima is feminine, but feminine nouns that begin with a stressed a take el in the singular to avoid the clash of two a's: el ánima, el agua.

Pronunciation

Respellings such as a-BEE-a are rough guides for an English speaker, with the stressed syllable in capitals. Trust the recording over the respelling.

Sounds to watch

  • tienen: two syllables, TYEH-nen.
  • propia: two syllables, PRO-pya.
  • áspero acento: stress the first syllable of áspero and link the two words, AS-pe-roa-SEN-to.
  • cuestión: kwes-TYON.
  • ánima: stress on the first syllable, A-ni-ma.

6Un gitano corpulento, de …show text

Un gitano corpulento, de barba montaraz y manos de gorrión, que se presentó con el nombre de Melquíades, hizo una truculenta demostración pública de lo que él mismo llamaba la octava maravilla de los sabios alquimistas de Macedonia.

A heavy gypsy with an untamed beard and sparrow hands, who introduced himself as Melquíades, put on a bold public demonstration of what he himself called the eighth wonder of the learned alchemists of Macedonia.

7Fue de casa en …show text

Fue de casa en casa arrastrando dos lingotes metálicos, y todo el mundo se espantó al ver que los calderos, las pailas, las tenazas y los anafes se caían de su sitio, y las maderas crujían por la desesperación de los clavos y los tornillos tratando de desenclavarse, y aun los objetos perdidos desde hacía mucho tiempo aparecían por donde más se les había buscado, y se arrastraban en desbandada turbulenta detrás de los fierros mágicos de Melquíades.

He went from house to house dragging two metal ingots and everybody was amazed to see pots, pans, tongs, and braziers tumble down from their places and beams creak from the desperation of nails and screws trying to emerge, and even objects that had been lost for a long time appeared from where they had been searched for most and went dragging along in turbulent confusion behind Melquíades’ magical irons.
