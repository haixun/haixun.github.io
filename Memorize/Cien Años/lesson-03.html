Macondo era entonces una aldea de veinte casas de barro y cañabrava construidas a la orilla de un río de aguas diáfanas que se precipitaban por un lecho de piedras pulidas, blancas y enormes como huevos prehistóricos.

Grammar and expressions

era: the describing past

era means "was". It is the imperfect of ser (to be), the tense for how things were, with no beginning or end in view. Compare llevó in sentence 1, a single finished event. The whole paragraph moves between these two past tenses: imperfect for background and habits, preterite for events.

se precipitaban

The ending -aban is the imperfect for "they" of an -ar verb: the waters were rushing, used to rush. The little word se goes with certain verbs to show the subject is acting on itself: precipitarse, to hurl oneself down.

de for material and belonging

casas de barro y cañabrava: houses made of mud and wild cane. un río de aguas diáfanas: a river with clear waters. un lecho de piedras: a bed of stones. English uses three different constructions; Spanish uses de for all of them.

Adjectives follow the noun and copy its ending

casas ... construidas, aguas diáfanas, piedras pulidas, blancas y enormes: all feminine plural, so the adjectives end in -as (or -es for adjectives like enorme that have one form for both genders). huevos prehistóricos is masculine plural: -os. construidas and pulidas are past participles (built, polished) doing the job of adjectives.

que and como

que after a noun means "that" or "which": aguas ... que se precipitaban, waters that rushed down. como means "like": como huevos prehistóricos.

Pronunciation

Respellings such as a-BEE-a are rough guides for an English speaker, with the stressed syllable in capitals. Trust the recording over the respelling.

Linking words

Spanish is spoken in unbroken chains. When one word ends in a vowel and the next begins with one, they flow together with no gap: una aldea sounds like u-nal-DE-a, de aguas like DEA-gwas, la orilla like lao-REE-ya. Do not stop between words the way careful English does.

New letter sounds in this sentence

  • gu + a is "gw": aguas A-gwas.
  • hu + vowel is "w": huevos WEH-bos (silent h, v like b).
  • rr rolled: barro. Initial r rolled: río RREE-o, two syllables.
  • ñ: cañabrava ka-nya-BRA-ba.
  • ei glides: veinte BEYN-te.
  • prehistóricos: the h is silent, so pre-is-TO-ri-kos.
  • diáfanas: stress on the accented á, DYA-fa-nas.

3Three adjectives describe the stones. Which?
pulidas (polished), blancas (white), enormes (enormous).
4Why era here but llevó in sentence 1?
era describes a lasting state (imperfect); llevó reports one completed event (preterite).

On the bank of a river.
