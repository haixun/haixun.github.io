The first sentence is one of the most famous in any language. It looks forward to an execution and back to a childhood afternoon in a single breath. Today you finish it.

MUCHOS años después, frente al pelotón de fusilamiento, el coronel Aureliano Buendía había de recordar aquella tarde remota en que su padre lo llevó a conocer el hielo.

Many years later, as he faced the firing squad, Colonel Aureliano Buendía was to remember that distant afternoon when his father took him to discover ice.

Grammar and expressions

había de recordar: "was to remember"

haber de + an infinitive is a slightly solemn way of saying that something is bound to happen. había is a past form of haber, so había de recordar means "was to remember", "would one day remember". The narrator already knows the colonel's future. The infinitive, the dictionary form of a verb, always ends in -ar, -er or -ir: recordar, conocer.

aquella tarde remota

Spanish has a special word for "that" when the thing is far away in space or time: aquel (masculine), aquella (feminine). tarde (afternoon) is feminine, so both words around it take feminine forms: aquella tarde remota. Notice that the describing word remota comes after the noun. That is the normal position in Spanish.

en que

Literally "in which". After a time word it does the job of English "when": aquella tarde en que..., "that afternoon when...".

lo llevó: the pronoun goes first

llevó means "he took". The ending -ó marks a single completed action in the past by he, she or it. Spanish usually leaves out the word "he": the verb ending says it. lo means "him", and it stands before the verb: su padre lo llevó, "his father him took".

a conocer el hielo

conocer is "to become acquainted with". llevar a alguien a conocer something is to take someone to see it for the first time. Spanish says el hielo, "the ice", where English says just "ice".

Pronunciation

Respellings such as a-BEE-a are rough guides for an English speaker, with the stressed syllable in capitals. Trust the recording over the respelling.

h is always silent

había is a-BEE-a, hielo is YEH-lo. Never breathe an English h.

ll sounds like English y

aquella a-KEH-ya, llevó yeh-BO.

b and v are the same sound

Both are a soft b. llevó has a b-sound in the middle, and había the same one.

r and rr

A single r inside a word is one quick tap of the tongue, like the middle sound of American "butter": coronel, padre, tarde. At the start of a word, and whenever it is written rr, it is rolled: recordar, remota. If you cannot roll it yet, use a firm tap and keep going. It will come.

c and qu

c is k before a, o, u or a consonant: coronel, recordar. Before e or i it is s in Latin America and th (as in "think") in most of Spain: conocer is ko-no-SER or ko-no-THER. Listen to which one the narrator uses and copy it. qu is always a plain k, with the u silent: que keh, aquella.

-ía is two syllables

The accent on the í keeps it apart from the a: había a-BEE-a, Buendía bwen-DEE-a.

Check yourself

Answer aloud or on paper first, then open the answer.

1Who took whom to see the ice?
His father took him, the future colonel: su padre lo llevó. lo = him.
2What does había de recordar mean, and what would plain "remembered" lose?
"Was to remember." It tells you the remembering lies in the future of the story, as fate.
3Why aquella and remota, both ending in -a?
They agree with tarde, a feminine noun.
4Which letter is silent in hielo and había?
The h.
5How many syllables in Buendía, and which is stressed?
Three: Buen-DÍ-a, stress on dí.
6Translate: su padre lo llevó a conocer el hielo.
His father took him to see (to discover) ice.
