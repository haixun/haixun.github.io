Grammar and expressions

Melquíades, otra vez, trató de disuadirlo

otra vez, "again". tratar de + infinitive, "to try to": you met its gerund in tratando de desenclavarse. And disuadirlo is the very word that ended Úrsula's sentence in paragraph 1: no consiguió disuadirlo.

Pero terminó por aceptar

terminar por + infinitive: "to end up doing". The subject is still Melquíades.

a cambio de la lupa

a cambio de, "in exchange for". In paragraph 1 the same idea used the verb: cambió su mulo ... por los dos lingotes imantados. Now the ingots themselves go back as part of the price.

Úrsula lloró de consternación

de gives the cause of an emotion or a bodily reaction: wept with dismay. Later: temblando de fiebre.

había acumulado, había enterrado

The pluperfect, "had done": había + participle. It reaches back before the story's present: her father had accumulated the coins, and she had buried them. enterrado is the opposite of desenterrar in paragraph 1: en- + tierra.

su padre, ella

su padre here is her father. Spanish normally leaves subject pronouns out; ella is stated because the subject changes from her father to her.

para invertirlas

las = las monedas (feminine plural), attached to the infinitive as in mencionarlas.

2What did he accept for the magnifying glass?

3Where did the money come from?
From a chest of gold coins that Úrsula's father had saved over a lifetime of hardship.
4Where had Úrsula kept it, and why?
Buried under the bed (debajo de la cama), waiting for a good opportunity to invest it.

Had buried.

las monedas, the coins.
