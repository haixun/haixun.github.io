Grammar and expressions

Every noun has a gender

Spanish nouns are either masculine or feminine, and the words around them change to match. año (year) is masculine, so "many years" is muchos años, with the masculine plural ending -os. With a feminine noun it would be muchas, which you will meet in sentence 3 (muchas cosas).

Time first, then después

después means "later" or "afterwards". The amount of time comes first, exactly as in English: muchos años después, "many years later".

al = a + el

frente a means "facing". When a (to, at) meets el (the, masculine) the two always fuse into al. So frente al pelotón is "facing the squad".

Nouns joined by de

English stacks nouns: "firing squad". Spanish reverses the order and puts de (of) between them: pelotón de fusilamiento, literally "squad of shooting". fusilamiento comes from fusil, "rifle". You will see this X de Y pattern more than thirty times in the paragraph.

Pronunciation

Respellings such as a-BEE-a are rough guides for an English speaker, with the stressed syllable in capitals. Trust the recording over the respelling.

Five vowels, five sounds

Each vowel has one short, clean sound. It never slides into another vowel and never weakens to "uh".

  • a as in father: años, casa
  • e as in bet: frente, de
  • i as in machine: fusilamiento
  • o as in more, cut short: muchos, pelotón
  • u as in rule: muchos, fusilamiento

English speakers tend to say "MOO-chose" and "duh". Aim for MOO-chos and deh.

Where the stress falls

  1. If a word ends in a vowel, n or s, stress the second-to-last syllable: muchos MU-chos, años A-ños, frente FREN-te, fusilamiento fu-si-la-MIEN-to.
  2. If it ends in any other consonant, stress the last syllable: coronel co-ro-NEL, recordar re-cor-DAR.
  3. A written accent overrides both rules and simply marks the stressed vowel: después des-PUÉS, pelotón pe-lo-TÓN.

The vocabulary reference shows every word split into syllables with the stressed one in capitals, so you never have to guess.

Two vowels that share a syllable

When i or u sits next to another vowel, the two glide together in one syllable: después is two syllables (des-PWES), and fusilamiento has five (foo-see-la-MYEN-to), not six.

ñ and ch

ñ is the "ny" of canyon: años A-nyos. Take care with this one: it is a different letter from n. ch is always the "ch" of church: muchos.

Check yourself

1Which syllable is stressed in pelotón, and how do you know?
The last: pe-lo-TÓN. The written accent marks it. Without the accent, a word ending in -n would be stressed on the second-to-last syllable.
2Which syllable is stressed in fusilamiento? There is no accent mark to help.
fu-si-la-MIEN-to. It ends in a vowel, so the stress falls on the second-to-last syllable; ie glides together as one syllable.
3Which two words are hidden inside al?
a (to, at) + el (the).
4Why is it muchos años and not muchas años?
año is a masculine noun, so the adjective takes the masculine plural ending -os.
5Put into English: frente al pelotón de fusilamiento.
Facing the firing squad.
6Say "many years later" in Spanish without looking.
Muchos años después.

Review

Memorise

Back-chaining. Long phrases are easiest to learn from the end. Say each line five times, then add the piece in front of it:

  1. fusilamiento
  2. de fusilamiento
  3. pelotón de fusilamiento
  4. frente al pelotón de fusilamiento
  5. Muchos años después, frente al pelotón de fusilamiento
