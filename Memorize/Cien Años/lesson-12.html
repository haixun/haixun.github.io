A warning ignored, and a bad trade

Two sentences of plain plot: Melquíades warns him, he does not listen, he trades away the family's animals. The grammar is lighter here, a good moment to pick up speed.

10

11

Pero José Arcadio Buendía no creía en aquel tiempo en la honradez de los gitanos, así que cambió su mulo y una partida de chivos por los dos lingotes imantados.

But José Arcadio Buendía at that time did not believe in the honesty of gypsies, so he traded his mule and a pair of goats for the two magnetized ingots.

Grammar and expressions

que era un hombre honrado

An aside between commas, introduced by que ("who"). era describes his character, so imperfect.

le previno

le means "to him" (José Arcadio); it stands before the verb, like lo. previno is the irregular preterite of prevenir, "to warn". lo is the direct object (he took him); le is the indirect object (he gave a warning to him).

«Para eso no sirve»

Word for word: "For that not it-serves". servir para is "to be good for"; eso is "that" in the sense of "that purpose". no always goes directly in front of the verb. Putting Para eso first gives it the stress: for that, it is no use. sirve is present tense, because he is speaking.

Pero ... no creía en ...

pero = "but". creer en = "to believe in". no creía is imperfect: it was his standing attitude. en aquel tiempo, "at that time", uses aquel, the masculine of aquella.

la honradez de los gitanos

honradez (honesty) is the noun made from honrado (honest), which you met one sentence earlier. Many qualities end in -ez and are feminine.

así que cambió ... por ...

así que = "so", "and so". cambiar A por B = "to exchange A for B": cambió su mulo y una partida de chivos por los dos lingotes imantados. una partida de is a batch or lot of something. imantados, "magnetised", comes from imán, the magnet of sentence 5.

Pronunciation

Respellings such as a-BEE-a are rough guides for an English speaker, with the stressed syllable in capitals. Trust the recording over the respelling.

r after n is rolled

honrado and honradez: the h is silent and the r, coming after n, is rolled like an initial r: on-RRA-do, on-rra-DES.

Stressed endings

  • honradez ends in z, so the last syllable is stressed.
  • cambió: two syllables, kam-BYO.
  • creía: three syllables, kre-EE-a.
  • así: a-SEE.
  • previno: pre-BEE-no.

ch

chivos CHEE-bos: ch as in church, v as b.

Delivery

The narrator pauses before the quotation. Make Para eso no sirve short and flat, the voice of a man stating a fact.

1What kind of man was Melquíades?
un hombre honrado: an honest man.
2What did he warn?
«Para eso no sirve»: it is no good for that.
3Why did José Arcadio not listen?
He did not believe in the honesty of gypsies: no creía ... en la honradez de los gitanos.
4What did he give, and what did he get?
He gave his mule and a lot of goats (su mulo y una partida de chivos) for the two magnetised ingots (los dos lingotes imantados).
5Where does no go in a Spanish sentence?
Immediately before the verb (and before any pronoun attached in front of it).
6Which earlier word is imantados related to?
imán, magnet (sentence 5).

Memorise

Honest ↔ honesty. The two sentences are hinged on one word family: Melquíades was honrado, but José Arcadio did not believe in la honradez of gypsies. Remember the hinge and you have both sentences' middles.

For the trade, picture a pair of scales: on the left pan a mule and goats, on the right two bars of metal, with por as the pivot.
