Evidence file

Seed-stage evidence: protein and antibody design companies

An evidence file, not an essay. It records, for roughly 45 AI/computational protein, antibody, binder and enzyme design companies, what each one actually had in hand at the moment of its first institutional financing — plus the amount, the investors, and the founders' credentials. Compiled 2026-11-11. Method: primary sources first — SEC EDGAR (S-1 and 10-K documents, and Form D primary_doc.xml filings, which give the amount actually sold and the date of first sale and proved the most reliable source of all), UK Companies House, the NCBI/PubMed E-utilities API for the publication record, the University of Washington Institute for Protein Design's own spinout list, and company press releases and websites — with trade press and aggregators used only where nothing better exists. Every figure is labelled independently verifiable (SEC filing, Companies House, PubMed), company-reported (press release or company site, i.e. primary but self-reported), or secondary (trade press and aggregators). Gaps are written as "not found" rather than estimated. Limits: the session's web-search budget (200 calls, shared with a parallel research agent) was exhausted three times, so several fields — notably pre-seed amounts for companies that never issued a press release, and founder PhD-lab affiliations for the smaller companies — remain open; businesswire.com and fiercebiotech.com return HTTP 403 to automated fetching, so some company-issued releases are recorded via trade-press carries; and aggregator totals (Crunchbase, PitchBook, Tracxn, Seedtable, Dealroom) frequently disagree with each other and with primary sources, in which case both figures are recorded. This file feeds a synthesis document and is intended to be appended to.

The short version

  1. The classic seed is the exception, not the rule, in this space. Of the companies here with a documented first institutional round, the first round was labelled "Series A" — not seed — in at least 12 cases (Icosavax, Neoleukin, Outpace, Vilya, Seismic, Diagonal, Deep Apple, Arzeda, Jura, Manifold's $40M, Infinimmune's $75M, Scala's $16M follows a seed). Several were created inside a venture firm and never had a seed at all: Generate Biomedicines (founded inside Flagship as "Flagship VL56, Inc."; the S-1's capital structure contains only Series A, B and C — no seed class exists), Xaira Therapeutics (">$1B committed" at launch, jointly incubated by ARCH and Foresite Labs), Seismic Therapeutic ($101M Series A as first round, Polaris/Lightspeed, Feb 2022), Deep Apple Therapeutics ($52M Series A, created and incubated by Apple Tree Partners, Dec 2023), Diagonal Therapeutics ($128M launch financing, Atlas/BVF, Apr 2024), Abiologics and Prologue Medicines (both Flagship Pioneering creations).
  2. The modal "what existed at seed" for the AI-first companies is ladder level 3: a model, computational benchmarks, no wet lab. Chai Discovery raised ~$30M on a team plus an unreleased structure-prediction model; Latent Labs, Profluent, Cradle, Bioptimus, 310.ai and Diffuse Bio all raised on software/model and credentials. Bioptimus raised a $35M seed with no product at all — the company was announced on the day the round was announced.
  3. The modal "what existed at seed" for the academic protein-design spinouts is ladder level 2 or 4: a Nature/Science/Cell paper from the founders' lab that functioned as the founding asset, sometimes with in vitro validation already published. Vilya licensed the platform and molecules from a specific Cell paper on membrane-traversing macrocycles; Neoleukin was built on the Nature Neo-2/15 paper; Archon on the Baker-lab "antibody cage" work; Skape Bio on a Nature paper on de novo miniproteins targeting GPCRs. The paper, not company data, was the asset.
  4. Non-dilutive money came first for several Institute for Protein Design spinouts. Archon Biosciences had roughly $7M in grants before its $20M seed (secondary). A-Alpha Bio layered NSF and Gates Foundation grants on top of a $2.8M seed. Icosavax's first equity was a $0.6M "Series 1" in January 2019 (SEC-verifiable), nearly two years after incorporation, and it later took a $10M Gates Foundation grant.
  5. Announced "seed" and "Series A" numbers are routinely tranched, and SEC filings show it. Monod Bio's announced "$25M seed" (Aug 2022) is two Form D filings: $6.0M sold with first sale 2021-11-29, then $24.2M sold with first sale 2022-06-10. Icosavax's announced "$51M Series A" (Oct 2019) was $25.9M in Aug 2019 plus $21.0M in Feb 2021. Vilya's "$50M Series A" was topped up by $21M in Jun 2024. Aqemia's "€60M Series A" was €30M in Oct 2022 plus €30M in Jan 2024.
  6. Founder pattern: a famous academic is almost always a scientific founder, never the CEO. David Baker (2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry) is scientific co-founder/advisor at Arzeda, A-Alpha Bio, Vilya, Archon, Monod, Neoleukin and Icosavax; in every case the CEO is someone else — usually a postdoc or graduate student from his lab (Zanghellini, Younger, Lazarovits, Silva, Bahl) or an outside operator (Simpson at Icosavax, Drachman at Neoleukin). George Church plays the same role at Nabla Bio, Manifold Bio, Dyno and Jura. Tim Springer and Jeff Ravetch play it at Seismic.
  7. The largest first rounds in the sector have no biology in them at all. Periodic Labs raised a $300M "founding round" led by a16z (Sep 2025) for AI in the physical sciences, on the strength of founders who co-created ChatGPT and DeepMind's GNoME — ladder level 1, team and idea only. This is the clearest available data point that the first-round size tracks founder provenance, not assets.
  8. In-hand evidence is inversely correlated with round size, and the three best-documented cases make the point starkly. Absci was at ladder level 4 with revenue declared on its own Form D and raised $3.66M (first sale 2016-04-06). Latent Labs was at ladder level 1–2 — first sale 28 days after incorporation, no product, no model, no lab — and raised $10M. Xaira was at level 3 resting on a 40-day-old bioRxiv preprint and raised >$1B. Capital follows the pedigree of the founders and the reputation of the published method, not the state of the company's data.
  9. A fourth origin category is needed beyond classic seed / venture creation / academic spinout: the corporate-lab spinout. Isomorphic Labs was wholly funded by Alphabet for ~3.5 years and its first round of any kind was a $600M "first external funding round" (Mar 2025). EvolutionaryScale and Latent Labs were team spin-outs from Meta FAIR and DeepMind respectively. In all three the founding publication — ESM, AlphaFold2, AlphaFold2 again — was a corporate paper, not an academic lab's. The venture-creation firm and the corporate AI lab have displaced the university tech-transfer office as the origin of these companies, and the brief's bucket (c), "academic spinout with non-dilutive grants first," fits none of Generate, Xaira, Absci, Isomorphic, EvolutionaryScale or Latent Labs.
  10. "Seed" as a label has detached from "seed" as an amount. EvolutionaryScale's first round was called a seed at >$142M — fourteen times Latent Labs' $10M seed, and a Series B by any economic measure. In the other direction, Absci's institutional "Series A" sold $3.66M. The label tells you about the investors' self-presentation, not the stage.
  11. Two exits and two consolidations have already happened in this cohort. Icosavax → AstraZeneca (2024); PvP Biologics → Takeda (2020); Neoleukin → merged into Neurogene (2023) after a clinical failure; and EvolutionaryScale is "now part of Biohub" — its own website says so, all three co-founders now hold Biohub titles, and the ESM models are now released openly by Biohub, but the transaction date, structure and terms are nowhere disclosed. Generate Biomedicines IPO'd on Nasdaq (GENB) in February 2026; Absci has been public since July 2021.

Company detail

The first six sections below rest mainly on SEC filings (the Generate S-1, the Absci S-1 and FY2025 10-K, and Form D primary documents for Absci and Latent Labs), UK Companies House, and the NCBI/PubMed API — the strongest evidence in this file. Note one structural finding from them: a fourth origin category is needed beyond classic seed / venture creation / academic spinout — the corporate-parent-funded spinout (Isomorphic) and the corporate-AI-lab team spinout (EvolutionaryScale, Latent Labs). Three of these six were founded on a corporate lab's publications (AlphaFold2, ESM, AlphaFold2 again), not an academic lab's.**

Notes on evidence

  • SEC Form D is the single most useful primary source for this question and is underused: it gives the issuer's year of incorporation, the executive officers and directors at the time, the total offering, the amount actually sold, the date of first sale — which is usually months before the press release — and sometimes a revenue range. It produced the Monod tranching, the AI Proteins $11.3M-of-$18.2M figure, the Absci revenue declaration, and Latent Labs' "28 days after incorporation." It is only available for US issuers that file, and several US companies here — Chai Discovery, Nabla Bio, Dyno Therapeutics, Periodic Labs, Xaira Therapeutics, Generate Biomedicines and EvolutionaryScale — returned no matching company in EDGAR's search, so either they filed under a different legal name or relied on §4(a)(2) without filing. Flagship- and ARCH-created companies appear not to file at all, which means the venture-creation model is systematically less documented in public filings than the classic seed model — a structural bias worth remembering when reading any funding dataset for this sector.
  • An S-1 is the best thing that can happen to this kind of research. The Generate S-1 and the Absci S-1/10-K yielded exact per-closing amounts, per-share prices, dated partnership payments, milestone ceilings, royalty bands, headcounts and accumulated deficits — none of which appears in any press release. Where a company in this file has never filed, the corresponding fields are thin, and that asymmetry is an artefact of disclosure law, not of the companies' actual histories.
  • Company news pages are systematically missing their own early financings. BigHat's begins Nov 2024; Aqemia's begins Nov 2025; LabGenius' and Dyno's carry no funding items at all; Basecamp's earliest item is a paywalled WSJ link. For seed-stage archaeology, the company's own site is often the worst source.
  • Business Wire returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching throughout, so several company-issued releases are recorded via trade-press carries or search snippets rather than the release itself.
  • One secondary aggregator — a protein-design funding tracker listing 84–87 deals — was used for discovery and for a small number of amounts where nothing better existed. It is labelled secondary everywhere it is used and should not be cited onward without verification; it was, however, the most efficient route to the 2025–2026 vintage and to the Chinese and Korean cohort.
  • The web-search budget for this session was exhausted three times (a 200-call session limit shared with a parallel research agent, refilling at roughly 100 calls per hour), which is why so much of this file rests on direct fetches of SEC, Companies House, PubMed and company-site URLs rather than on search. Per the brief, the key field — what existed at the first institutional round — was prioritised over peripheral fields, which is why founder PhD-lab detail is thinner than funding detail for the smaller companies.
