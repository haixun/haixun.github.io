AI and Drug Design

Two questions, worked through in some detail. How does the machinery actually function — structure prediction, protein design, and the filtering that stands between a designed molecule and a wet-lab experiment? And what did the companies in this field actually have in hand when they first raised money?

The financing study

What AI-for-drug companies had at their first institutional round, who their founders were, and how much they raised. Scope is protein and antibody design plus extracellular protein degradation.

  • What Gets Funded at Seed in AI Drug Design

    The synthesis. The first institutional round lands at the bottom two rungs of a seven-rung evidence ladder, and twice arrived before the paper the whole thesis rested on was published. Evidence in hand turns out to be inversely correlated with money raised. Announced amounts run about twice what is actually drawn. The median seed is $7.0M. And what moves a round by three orders of magnitude is a venture firm deciding to build around a laboratory — not the professor's participation.

Evidence files

The research behind the synthesis, one file per front, about 143,000 words in total. These are reference documents rather than essays: every figure is labelled company-reported or independently verifiable, gaps are written as “not found” rather than estimated, and each file ends with its own conflicts and sources.

On the evidence. The financing study rests largely on primary sources — SEC filings, Companies House, ClinicalTrials.gov, Europe PMC, FDA approval lists and domain registry records — because a search budget ran out early and pushed the work onto filings instead. Several of the strongest findings, particularly the gap between announced and drawn amounts, exist only for that reason. The same constraint means companies nobody named in advance were incompletely swept, and a substantial Chinese and Korean cohort is not covered at all. Every clinical percentage quoted anywhere in these documents is company-reported, open-label and small-n; the two most-cited efficacy figures in the degrader segment rest on about six and five participants respectively. Each document states its own limits.
