SEA-Eval: A Benchmark for Evaluating Self-Evolving Agents Beyond Episodic Assessment

arxiv.org/abs/2604.08988

Summary

A benchmark for self-evolving agents that runs sequential task streams rather than isolated episodes, with success rate and token consumption as primary metrics, to measure evolutionary gain, stability and convergence. Formalises the self-evolving agent around an 'Evolutionary Flywheel' architecture.

Our take

Somebody is building the measurement layer as a benchmark: equal success rates hide order-of-magnitude cost differences and non-convergence. It measures frameworks on public tasks, not a customer's own corpus, but it narrows our window.

Key numbers

  • Frameworks at comparable success rates (85%–96.7%) differ by up to 31.2× in token consumption on individual tasks (author-reported)
  • Under repetition of identical tasks one framework (GA) cuts token consumption by about 68% (R1→R3), while others show no monotone gain (author-reported)
