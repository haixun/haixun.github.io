Paper · 2026-05-13 · UMass Amherst; MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab; MIT

FlowCompile: An Optimizing Compiler for Structured LLM Workflows

author-reported

physical-plancompilesearchestimategeneralworkflow-compilationcost-quality

arxiv.org/abs/2605.13647

People Junyan Li, Zhang-Wei Hong, Maohao Shen, Yang Zhang, Chuang Gan

Summary

A compile-time optimiser for structured LLM workflows over a predefined execution graph. It profiles each sub-agent across models and reasoning budgets against pseudo ground truth from a reference model, composes the profiles through a structure-aware proxy that assumes independent sub-agents, and returns a reusable set of accuracy-latency trade-off configurations in one pass. Open-ended agents such as ReAct are explicitly out of scope; the base workflows are AFlow's.

Our take

The only published workflow-level cost model, and it validates against real labels, so it is the nearest research analogue of our physical-plan optimiser. Its independence assumption is exactly what correlated failure in enterprise document workflows breaks, and it configures a graph rather than deriving one.

Key numbers

  • Proxy vs measured over 20 configurations: Spearman 0.92 accuracy, 0.96 latency; 2.3-point accuracy error after bias correction (author-reported)
  • 3.4× average speedup at near-parity accuracy (6.4× on LiveCodeBench); 12.7× in latency-priority mode (author-reported)
  • Design-space ablation on HotpotQA expected utility: model only 69.7 → + reasoning budget 83.2 → + structure 88.4 (author-reported)
  • +7.9 expected utility over the strongest baseline, averaged over four benchmarks (author-reported)
  • Profiling about one hour per benchmark on 32 H100s; Qwen-3 0.6B–14B models only (author-reported)
