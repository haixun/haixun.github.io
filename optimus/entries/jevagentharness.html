Paper · 2026-10-15 · UIUC; Princeton; Google

JevAgentHarness: Benchmarking, Deploying and Training Decision Models That Share Control with LLMs

author-reported

physical-planweightssearchweight-updateestimategeneralcodecascades-routingcalibrationevaluationcost-quality

arxiv.org/abs/2610.21412
code: github.com/JevAgentHarness/JevAgentHarness

People Kunlun Zhu, Tao Feng, Xu Han, Heng Wang, Ling Yang, Xiangru Tang, Jiaxuan You, Heng Ji

Summary

Delegates an agent's multiple-choice decisions (which tool, which arguments, whether to stop) to a small decision model when its confidence exceeds a threshold fitted on the agent's own trajectories, leaving the LLM to decide below it. Introduces AgentDecisionBench and DecisionGym, trains AgentDecision-4B on states after mistakes, and evaluates with pre-registered non-inferiority tests and Holm correction.

Our take

The strongest evidence we have that the acceptance rule, not the component, is where value sits: isolated accuracy misranks models and a refitted threshold is worth 21.5 points. It is a cascade with calibration done properly, in the harness.

Key numbers

  • Spread across models: 5.1 pts isolated, 19.7 step-level, 57.5 task-level, with ordering inverted (author-reported)
  • Fixed 0.5 threshold −21.5 pts; delegating every tool choice −19.0 pts; fitted threshold +0.0 pts with 14.2% cost saved (author-reported)
  • 81.6% per-decision accuracy yielded 3.0% task success vs 88.0% for the LLM alone until the threshold was refitted (author-reported)
  • 81.1–89.0% cheaper and up to 2.5× faster on in-distribution tasks without losing 3+ points (author-reported)
  • Step-level accuracy on recorded runs ranks models with Kendall 0.84 against task success (author-reported)
