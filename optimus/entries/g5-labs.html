People Tim Kraska, Ziniu Wu

Summary

MIT CSAIL spin-out, out of stealth 15 September 2026, that captures intent once in a 'System Ontology', generates source code from it with third-party coding models (Claude Code, Codex, open-weight models), and claims every change is formally proven against the ontology. Sells to enterprise CIOs and architects, entering through legacy modernisation; forward-deployed engineers own the first project, then an annual platform licence, and the customer keeps the code.

Our take

The closest structural analogue to our proposal (declare intent, generate, certify against the declaration) and a plausible entrant: its founders come from the group that built Palimpzest and Abacus. Its 'formally proven' claim is unsubstantiated in public, and it targets deterministic code, where our certificate would have to be statistical.

Key numbers

  • $14M seed co-led by Pillar VC and Battery Ventures, Jeff Dean among angels (press)
  • Best agent passed functional tests on 91% of tasks but was working and secure on 25% (vendor-reported, citing a benchmark)
  • Across 718 firms adopting coding agents, PR open-to-merge time rose 49% (vendor-reported, citing a study)
