Summary

Separates the ability to produce useful harness updates (updating) from the ability to gain from them (benefit), across seven backbones on SWE-bench Verified, MCP-Atlas and SkillsBench. Updating is flat in base capability; benefit is non-monotonic, and weak models fail by not loading harness artifacts or by loading and not following them.

Our take

Gives two instruments a first engagement can run before any labels exist: skill-load rate (mechanical) and adherence drift (judged). It also says a cheap model can propose harness changes, which matters for optimiser cost.

Key numbers

  • Evolver spread at most 3.1 pp per benchmark; Qwen3.5-9B's updates match Claude Opus 4.6's (author-reported)
  • Skill-load rate 0.251 for Qwen3-32B against ~0.96 for strong models; Qwen3-235B loads at 0.961 but follows at 0.350 vs Opus 0.757 (author-reported)
  • Adherence drift across a trajectory: Qwen3-32B 0.52 → 0.13; Opus 4.6 0.89 → 0.80 (author-reported)
  • Weakest agent with its best evolver loses to strongest agent with its worst evolver by 18.6–35.2 pp on every benchmark (author-reported)
