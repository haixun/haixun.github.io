The Landscape

The map, assembled November 14, 2026 from our research notes and case studies. It organises the space rather than repeating the evidence; the companies, papers and products it names are in the catalog. Its job is to show what is occupied, what is contested, and what is empty. Revised November 17, 2026: the graveyard section was re-checked against primary sources, and the OpenAI and Databricks retreats updated.

The short version

  1. There are two routes to the same bet — compile or search — and both are occupied. The bet is that a human states what and a machine determines how.
  2. Three research schools sit underneath, and they barely cite each other: harness-search, workflow-compile, weight-update.
  3. The commercial layer has sorted into four tiers, and only one of them sells to enterprises without ML staff.
  4. Four teams shipped the broad version, and none survives as a standalone product. Re-read from primary sources, only one failed as a company; the automatic optimiser lives on as a feature inside platforms that already hold the data. That pattern is the most important feature of the map.
  5. In document processing specifically, extraction is commoditised and consolidating, the incumbents are impaired, and the frontier has taken the reading but not the accounting.
  6. One layer is empty across every part of this map: a trustworthy answer to whether the output is right.

1. The axis: compile or search

Compile Search
The human supplies intent, explicitly a metric
The machine supplies an implementation a candidate, then selection
Fails when the sketch was wrong, or the compiler's choices were the metric was wrong, and the search found that out
Research FlowCompile, PlanCompiler, Compiled AI ADAS, AFlow, Self-Harness, Meta-Harness, GEPA
Companies G5 Labs, XY.AI Labs Trajectory, Weco, Kiln
Proposals here the Optimus proposal the Problem-to-Outcome AI Platform proposal

The two are less opposed than they look — one compile system consumes a search system's discovered workflows as its input. Search produces the graph; compilation configures it.

And they share one dependency. The compiler needs an objective to choose among legal implementations; the search needs a comparator to choose among candidates. Neither escapes it, and nobody supplies it.

2. The research schools

School What it changes Representative work
Harness-update the scaffold, weights fixed ADAS, AFlow, Self-Harness, Meta-Harness, AHE, DemoEvolve
Workflow-compile compile-time artifacts, deterministic at run time FlowCompile, PlanCompiler, Compiled AI
Weight-update the model, absorbing the workflow SIA, Continual Harness, workflow-into-weights

Detail and numbers in harness research in the catalog. The organising idea worth carrying is the optimisation-target ladder — instruction prompts → structured context → workflow → harness code → optimizer code — where nobody works at more than two rungs at once, and moving up is an economic claim as much as a capability one: context interventions are paid at every call, structural ones once.

3. The commercial layer, in four tiers

Tier 1 — the frontier labs, retreating. OpenAI is ending fine-tuning entirely by 6 January 2027; Evals has been read-only since 31 October 2026, and Evals and Agent Builder shut down on 30 November 2026. Anthropic sells no fine-tuning at all, and is the only one of this tier a non-AI-native enterprise can use without an ML engineer, precisely because of that.

Tier 2 — the compute clouds, moving up-stack. Together, Baseten, Modal, Fireworks, Prime Intellect, Snowflake. All shipped customisation products in late 2026; all are SDKs for ML engineers priced per GPU-minute or per training token. They are suppliers, not competitors — and their economics favour the approach they can bill for, which is why none will build the thing that says "don't train."

Tier 3 — the customisation specialists. Trajectory (post-training and continual learning, RL only, sold to AI companies), Applied Compute (a services business wearing a platform's clothes, $350M at $3.25B), Fireworks (the incumbent, and the best compliance posture in the set), Thinking Machines (the layer Trajectory runs on).

Tier 4 — the one selling to enterprises without ML staff. Cohere: bespoke custom-model development under a BAA, deployed in the customer's own environment, ~85% of revenue from private deployments. No self-serve path, by design. It is the clearest existence proof that this buyer can be sold to, and the method is people plus private deployment rather than product.

And a fifth thing that is not a tier. Roughly $1.5B of capital — Anthropic with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman and Goldman — concluded this buyer needs consultants, and bought a services firm in May 2026. A product company here may end up selling to that rather than against it.

4. The graveyard, and what it really shows

The map's most important feature, because it marks ground that looks empty and is not. Re-read from primary sources on 14 November 2026, the record is weaker than "all four are gone":

Firm What it built What happened
Databricks Agent Bricks: Custom LLM Declare the task; auto-generate criteria and judges; compare prompt optimisation against fine-tuning; recommend a cost-optimised winner Marked "a legacy offering" between February and April 2026, users sent to SQL AI Functions. In October Databricks re-framed Agent Bricks as a code-first agent platform. Absorbed, not abandoned
TensorZero Open-source LLM-ops stack; "Autopilot" (March 2026) optimised prompts and models with an adaptive A/B test. $7.3M seed, 11.7k stars Wound down in June 2026, announced by its chief executive, remaining capital returned to investors. A company failure, but an LLM-ops one: Autopilot came eleven weeks before the end
Zenbase DSPy optimisation applied to existing code, by two DSPy contributors; about $500K Pivoted to systematic literature review in autumn 2025. Too small to say much about the market
DataRobot syftr Pareto search over about 10^23 retrieval and agentic configurations Shipped as a search feature in DataRobot's SDK and generative-AI playground in May 2026, still being extended

The lesson: the automatic optimiser survives as a feature inside a platform that already holds the data and the runtime, and dies as a product on its own.

Alongside it, the exit evidence: a filing shows one fine-tuning company was bought for $109.1M of which 85% was goodwill attributed to "assembled workforce" and $11.0M was technology on a two-year life. The category produced acqui-hires.

And the optimiser itself is free — MIT-licensed, an ICLR Oral, already inside five frameworks.

5. Document processing, where the wedge probably is

A different map, because the founders' advantage is here. Full detail in our document-processing research (not published).

Layer State
Extraction Commoditised. $0.50–$1.00 per 1,000 pages from at least three vendors; a frontier lab sells it with bounding boxes and per-word confidence at $2–4. ~100× spread across the category
The AI-native cohort Consolidating. Three acquisitions or exits in four days around this research; one company left the category entirely
The incumbents Impaired. One raised at $1.24B down from $2B on flat revenue and renamed itself; one was acquired; one's CEO says publicly that IDP may be "subsumed into an abstracted service"
The hyperscalers Conceding. Google discontinued ~20 legacy processors and rebuilt on Gemini, though several versions are documented as not meeting data-residency standards. AWS Textract's docs have no entry since 2022
The frontier models Have taken the reading, not the accounting. 93.0% per-field on a vendor's own benchmark — but 0.0 grounding F1, and on 358-page documents they complete about half, dropping whole records silently
Confidence and verification Empty
Configuration cost Unpriced. 20 to 80+ person-hours per document type, and enterprises have hundreds

6. What is empty

One thing, and it is the same thing from every direction on this map.

  • The compile route needs a quality objective and manufactures one from pseudo ground truth because it has no alternative.
  • The search route needs a comparator and gates on two runs, or on nothing until the end.
  • The self-improving systems predict their own regressions at roughly twice chance.
  • Document extraction has no independent evaluator at all: every benchmark is vendor-run and the publisher wins every one.
  • And three separate research groups have now written that the evaluator should sit outside the loop — none of them is building it.

The counterweight is timing. A benchmark for the measurement gap now exists, a twenty-author survey has written the research agenda, and the clouds have priced evaluation at zero before. An eighteen-month window is the working assumption.
